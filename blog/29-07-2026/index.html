Why the dimensions of the portal from 2001: A Space Odyssey may be physically impossible (Spoiler alert!)

        In 2001: A Space Odyssey, Bowman falls into a seemingly three-dimensional portal that has precisely a 1:4:9 aspect ratio. However, when he becomes immaterial, he learns that the aspect ratio extends beyond the three spatial dimensions we are familiar with (1:4:9:16:25...). Here's the problem: things get really messed up when you have more than three spatial dimensions. By the way, when I say "dimension", I mean mathematical dimensions, not "alternate realities".

        The x-axis is the number of spatial dimensions, starting at zero. The y-axis is the same for temporal dimensions. Let's break down this table.

Elliptic space (pink)

        This is where everything is... impossible. In elliptic space, there is either no "space" or no "time", though the meaning of both breaks down at this point. In the latter scenario, the universe is totally frozen. Since nothing happens inside it and there is no outside, it might as well not exist since it doesn't affect sentient beings and perception is reality. The former is weirder. In this scenario, all objects are static and constrained along any number of time-like axes. However, since there's no space, any objects in this reality will all do nothing. They will perpetually "interact" without changing state. It's just a static blob of energy spread out across all temporal axes. Concepts like "past" and "future" disappear, as do light cones.

Too simple (red)

        We're pretty sure intelligent life can't exist in these because one spatial dimension is not enough for any life, and because neural networks are impossible in two dimensions.

Where we think we are (white)

        Most people reasonably assume that they live in three spatial dimensions and one temporal dimension. If there was more than one time dimension, tachyons would be both possible and normal.

Tachyons only (yellow)

        If there is only one spatial axis but three temporal axes, objects can only move along a line, right? Yeah. But... they follow different time paths. Tachyons, by the way, are really interesting. They are theoretical particles that can move faster than light. But here's the really weird thing: If a particle is a tachyon, it cannot move slower than light. It gets weirder. Tachyons get faster when they have less energy, and it takes infinite energy to get them to the speed of light. They also obviously make weird things happen with relativity.

Unstable (blue)

        When you add spatial dimensions, atoms get really unstable. The inverse square law works only for three dimensions. Really, the law is F = 1/r^(n-1), where n is the number of spatial dimensions. Why? As rays of energy spread linearly, the area of influence increases quadratically.

        Because n-dimensional objects have (n-1)-dimensional surfaces, the strength of any force at certain distances changes drastically as you adjust the number of spatial dimensions. Even four spatial dimensions, in which objects have three-dimensional surfaces, is far too unstable for atoms to form. Atoms can form in two dimensions, but not one, both because the whole concept of "orbiting" breaks down and because all forces are constant. So, we know that there can't be more than three spatial dimensions available to normal matter, because we have an inverse square law and not an inverse cube law*.
        When you add temporal dimensions, things of course get weirder. With multiple time dimensions, different timelines can affect one another. Each sentient being only perceives one timeline, of course (the spread across timelines should create multiple distinct entities), but some timelines could intersect. This is very possible for our universe, and it could just have gone unnoticed, since you wouldn't be able to detect timelines crossing each other. Also, since paths could loop, retrocausality would be possible.

        *Actually, if space with extra dimensions were heavily warped to direct everything toward our three-dimensional "plane", we could have more dimensions, but no matter would be able to move along them due to conservation of momentum. Therefore, no being would be able to construct a four-dimensional object. We would still enjoy a strict inverse square law. However, gravity might still be able to propagate along other dimensions, which would explain its weakness.

Ultrahyperbolic (green)

        These are unpredictable simply because they're too complex for our computers to handle.

What if the portal's aspect ratio continued in our temporal dimension instead?

        Well, there are some problems with that. First, if an object has a finite temporal length, it spontaneously appears and disappears. Second, temporal length depends entirely on the temporal vector (one's trajectory through time when there is more than one temporal axis) of the observer. Third, if it continues infinitely, there would be infinite temporal axes, and things would definitely be messed up. Fourth, temporal length and spatial length measure entirely separate things (you can't convert meters to seconds). Finally, the sentient beings that designed it need their own temporal axis which they cannot control in order to be sentient (there cannot be thought without time), even if they perceive our temporal axis as spatial. Along their temporal axis, they could not reasonably control the length of the monolith.
