Why the dimensions of the portal from 2001: A Space Odyssey may be physically impossible (Spoiler alert!)

        In 2001: A Space Odyssey, Bowman falls into a seemingly three-dimensional portal that has precisely a 1\:4\:9 aspect ratio. However, when he becomes immaterial, he learns that the aspect ratio extends beyond the three spatial dimensions we are familiar with (1\:4\:9\:16\:25...). Here's the problem: things get really messed up when you have more than three spatial dimensions.

        The x-axis is the number of spatial dimensions, starting at zero. The y-axis is the same for temporal dimensions. Let's break down this table.

Elliptic space (pink)

        This is where everything is... impossible. In elliptic space, there is either no "space" or no "time", though the meaning of both break down at this point. In the latter scenario, the universe is totally frozen. Since nothing happens inside it and there is no outside, it might as well not exist since it doesn't affect sentient beings and perception is reality. The former is weirder. In this scenario, all objects are static and constrained along any number of time-like axes. However, since there's no space, any objects in this reality will all do nothing. They will perpetually interact without moving or doing anything else either. It's just a static blob of energy spread out across all temporal axes. Concepts like "past" and "future" disappear as well as light cones.

Too simple (red)

        We're pretty sure intelligent life can't exist in these because one spatial dimension is not enough for any life, and because neural networks are impossible in two dimensions.

Where we think we are (white)

        Most people reasonably assume that they live in three spatial dimensions and one temporal dimension. Regardless of how many time axes "exist" in our universe, no unique being can interact with more than one temporal vector. The reason for this is that the theoretical alternate versions of you are not "you", and neither are past, present or future versions. The concept of any being as a singular entity is deeply flawed. Think about it. You were likely very different ten years ago and will be very different ten years from now. Yes, there are similarities, but whatever makes you an individual has doubtless changed. The different versions often cannot be compiled into a singular entity because of their contradictory attributes. If there is an afterlife in which consciousness exists beyond death, which "you" gets to keep existing? Even that version will cease to exist quickly as it is changed by further experience. People are multitudes. Anyway, I'm rambling. This is a topic for another blog post. I should get on to the next area of the grid.

Tachyons only (yellow)

        If there is only one spatial axis but three temporal axes, objects can only move along a line, right? Yeah. But... they follow different time paths. Tachyons, by the way, are really exciting. They are theoretical particles that can move faster than light. But here's the really weird thing: If a particle is a tachyon, it cannon move slower than light. It gets weirder. Tachyons get faster when they have less energy, and it takes infinite energy to get them to the speed of light. They also obviously make weird things happen with relativity.

Unstable (blue)

        When you add spatial dimensions, atoms get really unstable. The inverse square law works only for three dimensions. Really, the law is $${F = \frac{1}{r^n}}$$, where $${n}$$ is the number of spatial dimensions. Why? As rays of energy spread linearly, the area of influence increases quadratically.

        Because multi-dimensional objects have multi-dimensional surfaces, the strength of any force at certain distances changes drastically as you adjust the number of spatial dimensions. Even four spatial dimensions, in which objects have three-dimensional surfaces, is far too unstable for atoms to form. Atoms can form in two dimensions, but not one, both because the whole concept of "orbiting" breaks down and because all forces are constant. So, we know that there can't be more than three spatial dimensions, because we have an inverse square law and not an inverse cube law.         When you add temporal dimensions, things of course get weirder. With multiple time dimensions, different timelines can affect one another. Each sentient being only perceives one timeline, of course, but some timelines could intersect. This is very possible for our universe, and it could just have gone unnoticed, since you wouldn't be able to detect timelines crossing each other. You could be interacting with an alternate... "you". Also, since paths could loop, retrocausality is theoretically possible. However, things would probably get screwed up with multiple temporal dimensions.

Ultrahyperbolic (green)

        These are unpredictable simply because they're too complex for our computers to handle.

What if the portal's aspect ratio continued in dimensions we perceive as temporal instead?

        Well, there are some problems with that. First, if an object has a finite temporal length, it spontaneously appears and disappears. Second, temporal length depends entirely on the path of the observer. Third, if it continues it infinite dimensions, there would be infinite temporal axes, and things would definitely be messed up. Oh, and there's a fourth one. The sentient beings that designed it need a temporal axis to be sentient, even if they perceive our temporal axis as spatial. Along their temporal axis, they could not reasonably control the length of the monolith.
